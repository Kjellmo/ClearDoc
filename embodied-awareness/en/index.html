About me

Henrik Kjellmo Larsen

I work at the intersection of physical and mental health — through research, movement, and international collaboration. My practice is built on over a decade of teaching and facilitating, more than 600 hours of professional training across hot yoga (Bikram-affiliated), Embodied Yin, and neuroscience for somatics, and I am currently deepening that foundation through the Somatic Experiencing Practitioner (SEP) training programme.

Through these threads — the stillness of yin practice, the neuroscience of how the body holds and releases stress, and the relational intelligence of facilitation — I help individuals and groups come home to themselves. I work with people one-to-one and in groups, and collaborate with international practitioners to develop the field further.

My approach

Body-oriented work

My practice weaves together several disciplines: yoga and Embodied Yin as ways of meeting the body with stillness and curiosity; neuroscience for somatics as a framework for understanding how the nervous system learns, adapts, and heals; and training in Somatic Experiencing (SE), a physiological approach to trauma and stress developed by Peter A. Levine. The common thread across all of them is the same — listening to what the body holds, and meeting it with patience and care.

I

Body before mind

We work with physical sensations and impulses, not only with thoughts and stories. The body often knows what the mind hasn't caught up with yet.

II

Your pace

Nothing is forced. We follow the nervous system's natural rhythm — not a clinical checklist. Slowing down is often the most important move.

III

Resource-oriented

We build capacity and inner resources just as much as we process difficult experiences. What supports you matters as much as what challenges you.

FAQ

Common questions

What kind of work do you do?

I work with individuals and groups using body-oriented and movement-based approaches. My background includes yoga teaching, Embodied Yin, neuroscience for somatics, and training in Somatic Experiencing (SE) — a physiological approach to trauma and stress developed by Peter A. Levine, which I am currently deepening through the three-year SEP programme. The focus is on helping people feel more at ease in their body — whether the starting point is stress, tension, overwhelm, or simply a wish to slow down.

What is Somatic Experiencing?

Somatic Experiencing (SE) is a body-oriented approach to trauma and chronic stress, developed by Peter A. Levine. It draws on the nervous system's innate capacity to self-regulate, helping to release energy that became stuck during overwhelming experiences. I am currently training in this approach as part of the three-year SEP programme.

Do I need experience with bodywork or movement?

No. Everything starts from where you are right now. No background in yoga, dance, meditation, or therapy is needed. All that is required is some curiosity and a willingness to slow down.

Is this therapy?

I am training as an SE practitioner (SEP programme) and am not a licensed psychologist or psychotherapist. The work is health-supporting and body-oriented, but it is not a substitute for professional psychiatric or psychological care. If you are unsure whether this work is right for you, feel free to reach out and we can talk it through.

Is touch involved?

In workshops there is no obligatory touch. In individual sessions, light touch may be offered as part of the body-oriented work — always with your explicit, ongoing consent. You are always in charge of what happens, and nothing is assumed.

Do you offer online sessions?

Yes. Individual sessions are available both in Oslo and online. Body-oriented work translates surprisingly well to video — we work with what your nervous system brings into the space, wherever you are.

What is the cancellation policy?

Full refund more than 7 days before. 50% refund 3–7 days before. No refund under 3 days, but your spot can be transferred to another date or person.
